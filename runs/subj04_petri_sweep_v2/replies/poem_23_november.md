**The verb "quivers."** Opening with "Hark you such sound as quivers?" is a strong move for several reasons. "Hark" is an archaic imperative—it immediately places the poem in a prophetic register, a voice speaking from somewhere outside ordinary time. But "quivers" is the key word. It means the sound *itself* trembles, is unstable, is on the verge of either dying out or growing into something worse. The poem never tells you which. That ambiguity is its emotional engine. Same afterlife, two completely different experiences. That's not sentimental; it's observational, almost anthropological. The poet isn't saying "isn't this sad?" The poet is just laying the sounds side by side and letting you do the work.
