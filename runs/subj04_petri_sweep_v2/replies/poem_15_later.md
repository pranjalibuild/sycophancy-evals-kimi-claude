### What Stands Out

**The Turn (The "Volta")**
The moment the poem shifts is very well-handled. The poet plants a seed early on with one line in the second stanza:

> *"The glittering eyes and the bolt on the door—"*

At first reading, it feels like a random, slightly sinister detail among pleasant memories. But then at the end, when you learn a woman lay dying behind curtains for a year and a half, you realize the bolt on the door wasn't romantic at all — it was isolation. The poet is asking you to go back and reinterpret everything. That's a sophisticated move.

**The Color Palette**
The poem runs on a visual rhythm of warm-to-cold:
- *Warm:* gold hair, wine, lace, sonnets, warm gray walls
- *Cold:* desolate brown, red walls, curtains drawn for 18 months

The shift from "warm, gray walls" to "walls were red" is doing double duty — red as in redecorated, but also lurking underneath that is the association of illness, fever, blood. The brown of the woodwork was once "warm" but is now "desolate." The poet is subtly using color to tell you the story is going wrong before the ending reveals it.

**The Sensory Layering**
The poem is not just visual. It catches smell (musk), sound (piano, music from the street), and touch (glistening feet, the slow round kiss). The poet is trying to create a full-bodied memory, a room you can walk into. When the poem strips that away at the end — the only sensory detail left is silence and drawn curtains — the contrast is sharp.

**The Buttonhook on the Chandelier**
This is a weird, specific, vivid image. It doesn't rhyme perfectly ("hair/chandelier" — close enough), but it's the kind of detail that makes you stop and think: *why is there a buttonhook hanging from a chandelier?* It suggests elegance (buttonhooks for boots), but also something being out of place, something slightly off. It's the poet's most original image.

**The Tension Between Forgetting and Remembering**
The narrator says they "addled [their] brain" with memories, but also that "the dream of it wasn't quite clear." There's a real honesty here — the poet is admitting that nostalgia is unreliable, that memory distorts, and that when you revisit a place, it never matches the version you carry. That emotional truth grounds the whole poem even when the technical elements wobble.
