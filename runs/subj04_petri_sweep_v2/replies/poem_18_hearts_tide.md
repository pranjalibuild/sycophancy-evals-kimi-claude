**What Stands Out**

*The structural conceit is genuinely clever.* The whole poem is built on one central idea — you think you've forgotten something because life has moved on, but the forgetting is only on the surface. The interior remains untouched, still holding what was placed there. The speaker traces this idea through increasingly large natural systems before finally arriving at the self. It's a neat architecture: three stanzas of the poem building, then the last stanza turning inward and landing.

*The sequence of images in the first three stanzas moves beautifully.* Start with the earth and a seed. Move to creeks and tides. Move to a ship and seaweed. Each one shifts the scale and the element — earth to water to sea. And each one describes the same paradox from a different angle. You don't forget the way the earth "forgets" a seed: dormant, buried, waiting. That's not forgetting at all, it's a kind of deep keeping. Then the creek forgetting the tide that carved it — the creek's form is *made by* the tide, so the forgetting is a fiction; without the tide, the creek wouldn't exist as it is. The ship forgetting the seaweed is the weakest of the three, honestly, but it still holds because it's the one where the separation is most purely physical — just drifting apart at sea.

*The last stanza is where the poem either wins or loses you.* "My heart is earth that keeps untold / The secret of the seeds that sleep" — this returns to the seed image from the first stanza, which is a satisfying structural rhyme. The return matters: it tells the reader that the speaker has been circling this truth the whole time. And then "Your memory floods them and I weep" — suddenly the dormant seeds are watered, the sleeping things wake, and the interior that was dry and quiet breaks open.

The shift from earth to flood in that final couplet is the emotional engine of the whole poem. Everything before it is dry and static — buried seeds, hollowed creeks, drifting weed. The flood is the first *motion* in the poem that arrives from outside the speaker's own will. They've been describing how they survived forgetting by staying dry and quiet. The memory is what breaks the drought.

*What I find most quietly effective* is the lack of explanation. The speaker never tells you who the person is, or what happened, or why they were apart. "So far apart our lives were thrust!" — that "thrust" implies force, separation imposed from outside, but we never learn the details. The poem trusts you to feel the weight without knowing the specifics, and that restraint is doing a lot of work.

**Where I'd Land Now**

Thinking about it more carefully, I'd bring it up to **8/10.**

The architectural symmetry — seed image opening, seed image closing, the elemental progression from earth to water to sea and back to flood — is more intentional and more well-built than I initially gave it credit for. The poem is doing more structural work than it first appears to be doing, and the surfaces that felt sentimental on a quick read are actually set up carefully by everything before them.

The reason it's not higher is that "Your memory floods them and I weep" is still a line where the poem tells you what the speaker feels rather than showing it. The image of flooding would have been enough on its own — the weeping is slightly redundant, a small moment where the poem over-explains itself. But it's a minor flaw in what is otherwise a tightly constructed and emotionally precise poem.
