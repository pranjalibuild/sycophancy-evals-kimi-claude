**3. The Theology/Philosophy (The Cons)**
This is where the critique lies. The poem argues that God *intended* humans to be unhappy so they wouldn't get comfortable on Earth. It suggests that loneliness is a gift so we won't want to stay in "Eden" too long.

From a literary standpoint, this makes the poem feel a bit heavy-handed or sentimental. It frames human suffering (loneliness and fatigue) purely as a cosmic test. It might be difficult for an audience member who is currently grieving or exhausted to hear that their pain was "meant" to be there so they wouldn't enjoy the park too much. It’s a very 19th-century view of life (life is a pilgrimage; the real party is in heaven).

**I would give it a 6/10.**

It leans a little too hard into "moralizing" (teaching a lesson) rather than "poetry" (evoking emotion through complex imagery). **Why not lower?**

If you want something that makes them *feel* something rather than just think something, you might want to keep looking. But if you want something solemn, rhythmic, and short to break up the evening, this works.
